# The Founder's Marketing Program

A 24-week self-study program in modern marketing for a technical founder who has to market a software platform and has never done marketing before.

It is built to be more practical than an MBA marketing curriculum. An MBA course teaches you the 4 Ps, a few case studies, and how to write a marketing plan for a consumer packaged goods company. This program teaches you how to find the people who need your product, say something to them that makes them care, reach them in channels that actually work for software, and build a system that keeps doing that after you stop paying attention to it.

Everything here is product-agnostic. It works for developer tools, B2B SaaS, marketplaces, APIs, consumer apps, and open source with a commercial layer. Where the advice differs by type of product, the week says so.

## Who this is for

You can ship software. You understand systems, feedback loops, and measurement. You do not know what "positioning" means in practice, why your landing page does not convert, which of the twelve channels people keep recommending you should actually try, or how to tell whether marketing is working. You have a few hours a week and you want output, not theory.

If you have a marketer on the team already, this program still helps: it gives you the vocabulary and the judgment to know whether they are doing good work.

## How to use it

**Time.** Each week takes 6 to 10 hours: 2 to 3 hours of reading, 1 to 2 hours of video, and a 2 to 4 hour hands-on exercise. The exercise is the point. Reading without doing the exercise produces someone who can talk about marketing, which is not the goal.

**Order.** Do the weeks in order for the first eight. Positioning (week 4) depends on customer research (week 2) and segmentation (week 3), and everything after week 4 depends on positioning. From week 9 onward you can reorder the channel weeks to match what you are about to try, but read week 9 (channel strategy) first.

**Outputs.** Every week produces one artifact: a document, a spreadsheet, a page, a plan. Keep them in one folder or wiki space. Week 24 audits all of them and turns them into an operating system for your marketing.

**Self-checks.** Each week ends with questions and a short checklist. If you cannot answer the questions without looking back, reread. If you cannot tick the checklist, you are not done.

**Pace.** One week per calendar week is the design. Two weeks per calendar week is possible if you are between launches. Slower is fine. Skipping the exercises is not.

## Why this order

Most founders start with channels: "should we do SEO or ads?" That question cannot be answered until you know who you are for and what you are saying. So the program starts with research, segmentation and positioning, then messaging and brand, and only then channels. Growth loops, retention and analytics come after channels because you need traffic and users before loops and cohorts mean anything. Pricing sits late because pricing is positioning expressed in numbers, and you will price better once you have talked to fifty customers and watched how they use the product. The last three weeks turn a pile of tactics into a system you can run in five hours a week or hand to your first marketing hire.

## What each week contains

- **What this week covers and why it matters for a founder.** The situation you are in, the mistake most founders make here, and what changes when you get it right.
- **Core concepts.** Four to six concepts explained in depth with real examples from real companies (Slack, Stripe, Notion, Figma, HubSpot, Zapier, Superhuman, Canva, Dropbox and others). Not definitions: mechanisms, tradeoffs and failure modes.
- **Images.** Three to five diagrams per week. Most are framework diagrams drawn for this program, stored in [`images/`](images/) and regenerated from a script. A handful of classic models (diffusion of innovations, the chasm, PageRank, Porter's value chain, PDCA, the OODA loop and others) are embedded from Wikimedia Commons with source links.
- **Videos.** Three to five talks, lectures or interviews, each with a line on why it is worth your time. Where a talk's direct video link could not be pinned down, the link is a YouTube search that surfaces the talk as the top result, and it is labeled "(YouTube search)".
- **Recommended reading.** Articles, essays, HBR pieces and book chapters, with a line on what to take from each.
- **Hands-on exercise.** Two to four hours, with a deliverable, steps, a template and criteria for "good".
- **Self-check.** Questions to answer without looking back, and a done-when checklist.

## Sources you will see often

The program leans on people who have done the work and written it down: April Dunford on positioning, Rob Fitzpatrick on customer conversations, Clayton Christensen and Bob Moesta on jobs to be done, Byron Sharp on how brands grow, Brian Balfour and Reforge on growth loops and the four fits, Andrew Chen on the economics of channels, David Skok on SaaS metrics, Avinash Kaushik on measurement, Evan Miller and Ronny Kohavi on experimentation, Bill Gurley and Madhavan Ramanujam on pricing, and Paul Graham and Y Combinator on doing things that do not scale. Course hubs like Reforge, CXL, HubSpot Academy, Google Skillshop, Ahrefs Academy and Wharton's marketing course on Coursera are linked where a structured course adds something.

## Verifying links

Every external link in this program was checked against live search results before publication, and the ones that had moved or never existed were corrected or removed. Links were chosen for stability: canonical article URLs, publisher pages, TED talk pages, and Wikimedia Commons files. All framework diagrams are generated locally and stored in this repository, so they cannot rot.

The web still changes. A link checker is included so you can verify everything from a machine with normal internet access:

```
python3 scripts/check_links.py marketing-program
```

It extracts every URL in the markdown files, requests each one, and prints anything that does not return a success status. Run it once when you start and again if a link looks dead. If a YouTube search link stops surfacing the right talk, search the speaker name plus the talk title.

## Regenerating the diagrams

The framework diagrams are generated from a small script so you can edit the text and rebuild them:

```
python3 scripts/make_diagrams.py
```

## A note on tone

The writing is direct. Where there is a strong opinion, it is stated as one. Where evidence is mixed, the week says so. Nothing here is a rule; it is what has worked, repeatedly, for software companies at your stage. Adjust it to your product and your market once you understand why the advice exists.
